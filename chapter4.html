PhD thesis · Chapter 4

Fragment Isotope Fingerprints Distinguish Biological Sources of Phytol

Multivariate classification of isotope patterns from ten bacteria, algae and plants

Overview

Molecules with an identical structure can be made by different organisms through different metabolic routes. Molecular average isotope values often cannot tell them apart. This chapter tests whether the fragment isotope patterns from Chapter 3 work as a fingerprint of where a molecule came from.

Phytol is built from isoprene units that are made by one of two evolutionarily distinct pathways, the methylerythritol phosphate (MEP) pathway or the mevalonate (MVA) pathway. These pathways are expected to leave different isotope signatures within the molecule.

Methods

I assembled a phylogenetically and ecologically diverse sample set of ten phototrophs. It includes three bacteria (Synechococcus sp. PCC 8806, Chlorobium limicola and Chloroflexus aurantiacus), green, brown and red macroalgae, and C3, C4 and CAM land plants. The cyanobacterium was additionally grown under controlled variations in temperature, light regime and growth phase to test how robust its fingerprint is. Phytol was extracted, purified and measured for 26 fragment isotope ratios. Patterns were compared with hierarchical clustering and non metric multidimensional scaling (NMDS), and the fragments driving the separation were identified with permutation based vector fitting.

Key results

  • Fragment isotope profiles are characteristic of the source organism and replicate measurements cluster together.
  • Phytol from two MEP pathway bacteria with very different physiologies shares a pattern that is distinct from MVA derived phytol in Chloroflexus aurantiacus.
  • Growth temperature, light and growth phase change the amplitude of the cyanobacterial fingerprint but do not erase the features that separate it from MVA phytol.
  • Among eukaryotes, differences track the way carbon is assimilated (for example C4 and CAM metabolism) more than the presence of a cytosolic MVA pathway.

Impact

Because the method relies on relative fragment to fragment patterns measured against one common standard, it needs no position specific reference material. That makes it transferable to other isoprenoids and compound classes, and to forensic questions where the origin of a compound has to be established from its isotope structure.
