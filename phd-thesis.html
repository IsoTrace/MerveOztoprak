PhD thesis · NIOZ and Utrecht University

Orbitrap Isotope Fingerprinting of Lipids

New mass spectrometry methods that read the isotope structure inside molecules, and what that structure reveals about where they came from

Overview

Every organic molecule carries an isotope fingerprint of the organism, pathway or process that made it. Conventional methods measure only the average over the whole molecule. In my PhD I developed Orbitrap mass spectrometry methods that measure carbon isotopes in many fragments of a molecule at once, at the nanomole amounts found in environmental samples.

The thesis focuses on phytol, the side chain of chlorophyll, and on its geological product phytane. It moves from method development on GC- and LC-Orbitrap instruments to applications in source attribution, metabolic pathway tracing and the analysis of rare multiply substituted isotopologues. Data processing was done in custom, openly shared R workflows.

Why it matters beyond geochemistry

The same principle that distinguishes phytol from a cyanobacterium and from a plant can distinguish a chemical made by one industrial route from another. Fragment isotope fingerprinting needs no compound specific reference standards and works at trace amounts, which makes it attractive for environmental forensics, chemical attribution, food and drug authenticity, and metabolic research.
