PhD research

Carbon Isotopes in Fragment Ions of Phytane

First natural abundance isotope measurement of a long chain hydrocarbon fragment, developed at Caltech

Overview

Phytane is one of the most common molecules in sediments and oils. It can derive from the chlorophyll side chain of photosynthesizers or from archaeal membrane lipids, and its carbon isotope composition has long been used to reconstruct past environments. Compound specific analysis only gives one average value for all twenty carbon atoms. I developed a GC-Orbitrap method to look inside the molecule instead.

Methods

Phytane was separated by gas chromatography and ionized by electron ionization on a GC-Orbitrap system. Two introduction modes were compared. In direct elution the analyte enters the ion source straight from the column. In the peak broadener (peak capture) mode the eluting compound is trapped in a reservoir and released slowly, stretching a peak of a few seconds into a stable signal of about an hour. The 13C/12C ratio of the diagnostic C6H13+ fragment ion (m/z 85) was then measured at high mass resolution, which separates the 13C isotopologue from interfering ions.

Raw scans were filtered and processed with custom R pipelines, errors were propagated and compared with the theoretical shot noise limit, and results were benchmarked against molecular average values from GC-IRMS.

Key results

  • Precision of better than 0.7‰ (standard error) and reproducibility of better than 1.3‰ (standard deviation, triplicates on consecutive days).
  • Only about 13 nmol of phytane needed per acquisition.
  • Measurement error approached the shot noise limit, the best precision physically possible for the number of ions counted.
  • Phytane derived from a cyanobacterium (Anacystis nidulans), from an archaeon (Halobacterium sp.) and from a commercial standard showed fragment differences consistent with their molecular averages, validating the measurement.

Impact

These were the first observations of intramolecular isotope differences in a long chain hydrocarbon at natural abundance. The work established Orbitrap isotope analysis as viable for sedimentary biomarkers, not only for small polar molecules such as amino acids, and laid the groundwork for multi fragment isotope analysis of phytol by LC-Orbitrap MS.
