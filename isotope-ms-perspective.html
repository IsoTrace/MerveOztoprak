Isotope Ratio Analysis on Bioanalytical Mass Spectrometers

Thesis introduction and an international perspective on a new generation of isotope tools

Overview

For decades, high precision isotope ratio measurements were the domain of dedicated gas source magnetic sector instruments. Bioanalytical mass spectrometers such as the Orbitrap, which are everyday tools in proteomics and metabolomics, can now be tuned to measure isotope ratios inside intact molecules. This opens isotope analysis to a much wider range of compounds and laboratories.

Together with colleagues from the growing Iso Orbitrap community I coauthored a perspective article in the Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry on this development. It explains why isotopes within molecules are informative for biology, chemistry and geology, reviews how electrospray and other soft ionization sources make polar and intact compounds accessible, and outlines where the field is heading.

Key themes

  • Why bulk and compound specific isotope values average out information that is stored at individual atomic positions.
  • How position specific and fragment specific isotope analysis on Orbitrap instruments recovers that information from nanomole amounts of material.
  • Why long chain apolar lipids such as phytol and phytane are an important but technically difficult target for these methods.
  • Where the field is heading, from biomedical and metabolic research to environmental and forensic applications.

Why it matters

Isotope fingerprints inside molecules can tell apart compounds that are chemically identical but were made by different organisms, pathways or industrial routes. That makes the approach relevant well beyond geochemistry, for example in food authentication, doping control, environmental forensics and the attribution of chemicals of concern.
