Earlier research

Reconstructing Eutrophication in Lake Seeburg

Lipid biomarkers in water and sediments of a shallow lake in Lower Saxony, Germany

Overview

Lake Seeburg is a shallow lake near Göttingen that has seen increasingly frequent cyanobacterial blooms because of ongoing nutrient input. The project used molecular biomarkers to reconstruct how the lake changed over recent decades and to identify indicators that managers can use for other lakes.

Methods

The inflow, the lake water and the uppermost sediment were sampled in 2018 and 2019, and two sediment cores of about 30 and 50 cm were analysed. Lipid fractions (hydrocarbons, sterols and fatty acids) were isolated and measured on a Thermo Trace 1310 GC coupled to a Quantum XLS Ultra MS with a ZB-5MS column, in full scan electron ionization mode (70 eV, m/z 50 to 800). Compounds were identified with published spectra and authentic standards, and grouped by principal component analysis.

Key results

  • Biomarkers fell into five groups, separating compounds delivered by the inflow from those produced in the lake by algae and bacteria.
  • C27 sterols mainly came from within the lake, while C29 sterols mainly came from the inflow.
  • In both cores the C27 to C29 sterol ratio was highest at the surface, recording rising in lake productivity and eutrophication.
  • Combustion derived PAH signals in the cores helped anchor the record to a documented local fire event and to land use history.

Impact

The ratio of C27 to C29 stenols, together with their stanol degradation products, was established as a practical molecular indicator for reconstructing eutrophication trends in shallow lakes, supporting environmental monitoring and lake management.
