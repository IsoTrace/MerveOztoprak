Earlier research

Biomarker Trends in Eutrophic Lake Seeburg

MSc research project on lipid biomarker signatures in sediment cores from a shallow lake in Lower Saxony, Germany

Overview

This study, carried out in collaboration with the University of Göttingen, reconstructed the environmental history of Lake Seeburg, a shallow and persistently eutrophic lake east of Göttingen. Sediment cores were analysed for lipid biomarkers, providing insights into eutrophication processes, anthropogenic impacts and natural ecological shifts.

Methods

Sediment cores were taken from the western shore of the lake and sliced into 15 horizons down to about 50 cm. Freeze dried sediments were analysed for total organic carbon, extracted with dichloromethane and methanol, desulfurised, derivatised (fatty acid methyl esters and trimethylsilyl ethers) and separated into hydrocarbon, fatty acid, alcohol and polar fractions by column chromatography.

Fractions were injected on a Thermo Trace 1310 GC coupled to a Quantum XLS Ultra MS, equipped with a Zebron ZB-5MS column. The GC oven was ramped from 80 °C to 320 °C at 5 °C/min and held for 30 minutes. Electron ionization mass spectra were acquired in full scan mode (70 eV, m/z 50 to 800). Compounds were identified with published libraries and reference compounds and quantified against an internal standard in Thermo Xcalibur. Depth profiles were compared with regional planning records and eutrophication data.

Key results

  • Depth dependent shifts in biomarker assemblages aligned with the nutrient input history of the lake.
  • C/N ratios and biomarkers of land plants and algae traced the changing balance between autochthonous and allochthonous organic matter.
  • A PAH signature indicative of a major hotel fire close to the lake in 1984 provided a time marker that allowed us to date ecosystem changes against the historical record of the area.
  • Cyanobacterial blooms were recorded both in recent sediments and in the early 19th century, while diols and keto-ols showed that Eustigmatophyceae were more prominent before the 1970s.
  • Tetrahymanol and other biomarkers indicated persistent water column stratification since the 1950s, when the inflowing Aue stream and the Seeanger wetland were first altered.
  • Integration with regional records revealed land use and water management decisions as major drivers of ecological change.

Impact

The project demonstrates how biomarker data can reconstruct the history of shallow lake ecosystems. Pinpointing the hotel fire with PAH biomarkers improved our interpretation of land use change, and showed that nutrient runoff from agriculture keeps driving eutrophication even after renaturation efforts. This integrated approach offers valuable tools for environmental monitoring and the management of eutrophic lakes.

The work contributed to a larger study of the lake by the Göttingen group, published in Organic Geochemistry.
