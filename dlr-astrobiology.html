Earlier research

Microbial Survival Under Space Radiation

Astrobiology internship at the German Aerospace Center (DLR), Cologne

Overview

How long can microorganisms survive the radiation environment of space or of a planetary surface? The answer matters for the search for life beyond Earth and for planetary protection, which aims to prevent spacecraft from carrying Earth microbes to other worlds.

Methods

  • Exposure of microorganisms to UV radiation simulating solar exposure, to ionizing radiation simulating cosmic rays, and to combined regimes.
  • Survival measured with colony forming unit counts, growth curves and metabolic assays.
  • DNA integrity assessed with gel electrophoresis and molecular markers of damage and repair.

Key results

  • Clear species specific differences in survival under UV and ionizing radiation.
  • Some organisms retained partial genomic stability under extreme irradiation, highlighting the role of DNA repair.

Impact

The results feed into planetary protection protocols and models of microbial survival on Mars or icy moons. For me, the internship built a solid foundation in microbiology and sterile work that I later used to culture bacteria for my PhD.
