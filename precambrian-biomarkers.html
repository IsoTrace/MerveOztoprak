Earlier research

Eukaryotes in Tonian Ecosystems

MSc thesis on biomarkers and inorganic geochemistry of the Empress 1A drill core, Western Officer Basin, Australia

Overview

The Neoproterozoic Era (1000 to 541 million years ago) saw the changes to Earth and life that ultimately led to complex multicellular organisms. Before the Cryogenian glaciations, eukaryotes appear to have played only a minor role in marine ecosystems, often explained by a lack of nutrients. Because well preserved molecular fossils from this time are rare, this idea had hardly been tested.

For my MSc thesis I studied exceptionally immature, indigenous biomarkers from the Tonian Empress 1A drill core of the Western Officer Basin and correlated them with geochemical parameters to link depositional environment, diagenesis and nutrient availability.

Methods

  • Sampling of 115 rock samples along the 1.6 km long Empress 1A core at the Geological Survey of Western Australia core library, covering all formations of the Buldya Group and four depositional environment types.
  • Exterior and interior separation of every rock with saws and clean tools, cumulative system blanks of combusted sand, and baked glassware throughout to monitor contamination.
  • Bitumen extraction by accelerated solvent extraction (Dionex ASE 200), desulfurisation and column fractionation.
  • GC-MS and multiple reaction monitoring GC-MS/MS on an Agilent 6890 GC coupled to a Micromass AutoSpec double sector mass spectrometer.
  • Major elements by XRF, and TOC, total nitrogen, δ15N and δ13C by elemental analyser IRMS in collaboration with the University of Washington.
  • Statistical analysis and cluster analysis in R, with mathematical blank correction and thermal maturity assessment.

Key results

  • Built an internally consistent dataset of indigenous biomarkers by screening contaminants with exterior and interior comparisons, maturity checks and blank correction.
  • Confirmed the unusual Tonian sterane signature, with about 99% C27 steranes, 1% C28 and virtually no C29, plus indigenous saturated and aromatic ergosteroids and cryostane.
  • Showed that eukaryotes played a subordinate role in all depositional environments of the core.
  • Linked ergosteroid occurrence to oxidising depositional environments.
  • New multiparameter correlations suggest that cryostane derives from aerobic heterotrophic reworking of organic matter.
  • δ15N data show that nitrogen was not limiting and that surface waters were oxygenated, pointing to phosphate as a possible limiting nutrient.

Impact

The study provides the most detailed record of biomarker, inorganic and isotope data for the Empress 1A core and narrows down why eukaryotes remained minor players before the Cryogenian. It also trained me in ultraclean trace level organic analysis, where blanks, controls and transparent data quality criteria decide whether a result can be trusted.
