Earlier research

Biomarkers in Precambrian Drill Cores

Molecular fossil analysis and contamination screening at the Australian National University

Overview

Ancient rocks preserve molecular fossils, or biomarkers, that can reveal which organisms lived in early ecosystems. Because these molecules occur in tiny amounts, separating genuine ancient signals from modern contamination is the central analytical challenge. During my MSc I worked on Precambrian core material from the Officer Basin in Western Australia in a lab that specialises in exactly this problem.

Methods

  • Fieldwork in Western Australia and sampling from the national core library.
  • Clean lab sample preparation and solvent extraction of rock fragments.
  • GC-MS with electron ionization (70 eV) for n-alkanes, hopanes, steranes and aromatic steroids, processed in Thermo Xcalibur.
  • Compound specific δ13C analysis (IRMS) of selected compounds and XRF for elemental context.
  • Maturity and syngeneity checks using sterane and hopane isomer ratios and procedural blanks.

Key results

  • Produced biomarker inventories for the studied core intervals together with maturity parameters.
  • Systematically flagged and excluded contamination signals, which made the remaining dataset more robust.
  • Contributed to biomarker discovery and palaeoenvironmental reconstruction in the group.

Skills gained

This project trained me in trace level organic analysis where blanks, controls and data quality criteria decide whether a result can be trusted. That mindset carries directly into regulated and forensic analytical work.
